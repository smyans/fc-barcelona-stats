What wins Barça games?

Every pass, shot, tackle and press from 524 league matches across the Messi era, used to answer one question: when Barcelona win, and when they drop points, what's actually different?

The short version Player stats Shot map Territory Win model Method

The short version

  1. It comes down to chances, not the ball. In wins, Barça created a median 2.14 xG and allowed 0.66. When they dropped points, it was 1.24 and 1.08. Possession barely changed: about two-thirds of the passes either way (66.5% in wins, 67.6% when dropping points).
  2. Dropped points happen around the opponent's box, not Barça's. When Barça drew or lost, they had more of the ball in the opponent's half, camped on the edge of the area against a deep defence. The model backs this up: holding chances equal, games where Barça had to force far more forward passes than the opponent went worse.
  3. The forwards defended. Barça attackers who pressed, tackled and won the ball back in midfield were more likely to be on the winning side. The high press wasn't just style; it shows up in the results.
  4. Midfield: keep it clean and switch it. Accurate passing and switches of play go with winning. Midfielders forcing it forward on the dribble go with dropped points, usually a sign the passing lanes were closed.
  5. Better finishing than the chances deserved, in all 17 seasons. Barça scored 1,347 goals from 1,053 xG. Messi alone is +148 (472 goals from 324 xG), but the rest of the squad is +145 too, a hint that Barça's chances were even better than an average-team xG model rates them.

Forwards decide games

The darkest blue column is the attackers'. Their xG is the single strongest player-level link with the result, followed by expected assists and shots. In wins, the typical Barça forward produced 0.40 xG per 90; in defeats, 0.22. With a front line this good, the question was rarely whether they would finish, and usually whether they got the chances.

A front line that defended

More surprising is how much of the attackers' column is about defending. Pressures, tackles, tackles won and ball recoveries in midfield by Barça's forwards all go with winning. The counter-press, winning the ball back high and straight away, is usually talked about as a style. Here it's measurably tied to results.

Midfield: control, switches and a warning sign

Midfielders' links are weaker, but a pattern emerges: accurate passing, switches of play and chance creation go with winning. The one stat that clearly goes the other way is progressive carries. When midfielders had to dribble the ball forward themselves, it usually meant opponents had shut the passing lanes, and those are the games Barça struggled in.

Defenders: almost no signal

Barça's defenders look the same in wins and defeats. Only switches of play show real evidence. This doesn't mean defenders didn't matter; it means their individual numbers don't capture it. Their job was to recycle the ball and keep the shape, and they did that in good and bad games alike.

Context matters: compare the World Cup

In the same analysis of the 2022 World Cup, pass completion was irrelevant and having more of the ball went with losing. For Barça, a team built on keeping the ball, accurate midfield passing matters a little. What wins depends on the team: there isn't one universal stat.

Where Barça's goals came from

63% of Barça's shots came from inside the box, but 86% of their goals did. A shot inside the area went in 22% of the time. From outside it was 6.4%, and that number is unusually high: opponents managed 2.6% from the same range. The difference is mostly one player. Messi scored 88 of Barça's 193 goals from outside the box, including 39 direct free kicks.

Finishing that beat the model every year

Barça scored more than their chances were worth in every one of the 17 seasons. The biggest gap was 2012/13 (+41 goals); the smallest were 2005/06 (+0.9) and 2020/21 (+4.0). Messi accounts for about half the total, but the rest of the squad beat their xG by a similar amount. That's partly great finishers, and partly that xG models are built on average teams: Barça's chances often came after pulling a defence apart, which the model can't fully see.

The defence was built on prevention

Switch to Shots conceded and the picture changes. Opponents averaged under one xG a game and scored about what those chances were worth (423 goals from 439 xG). Barça's defensive record didn't depend on goalkeeping miracles; it came from not letting teams shoot from good positions.

The surprise: dropped points happen in the opponent's half

You might expect Barça to drop points when they were pinned back. The opposite is true. When they drew or lost, Barça had more of their on-ball actions in the opponent's half (59.1% vs 56.6% in wins). The deepest red zone sits just outside the opponent's penalty area, in the middle of the pitch.

That's the picture of a low block: ten defenders packed around their own box, and Barça passing around the edge of it without getting in. In wins, Barça had slightly more of the ball inside the box and more in their own half, which means they were building patiently and then breaking through.

Part cause, part effect

Some of this is the score. Once Barça were 2–0 up, they could keep the ball in safe areas and let the clock run, so wins naturally include more possession deep in their own half. But the edge-of-the-box pattern in dropped points matches the model's finding about forced forward passing: against a deep, compact defence, more of the ball in the final third doesn't mean more danger.

Can the stats tell a win from dropped points?

A logistic regression compares Barça with the opponent on eight in-game stats, plus home or away. It's tested one season at a time: trained on the other 16 seasons, then asked to judge every match of the season it never saw.

What it learned

xG difference does most of the work. On its own it separates wins from dropped points well (AUC 0.81). Adding the other stats and venue improves that to 0.84. Beyond chances, three things clearly matter. Out-defending the opponent (more tackles, interceptions and blocks) helps. Playing at home helps: Barça won 83% of these home games and 64% away. And making many more progressive passes than the opponent hurts, once chances are accounted for. That's the low-block story again: when Barça had to keep forcing the ball forward without creating more, the game was getting away from them.

Possession and pass completion pull in opposite directions, and neither is clearly different from zero. At this level of dominance, how much of the ball Barça had tells you little about the result.

It knows when to be confident

When the model gave Barça a 95%+ chance of winning, they won 97% of those games. Below 30%, they won 18%. Its probabilities mean what they say, which is the main thing you want from a model like this.

What it can't see

In 77 of the 138 games where Barça dropped points, they still created more xG than the opponent. Football is low-scoring, and a 4.17-xG home defeat to Real Betis (3–4 in 2018/19) is the reminder. This model also uses stats from the match itself, so it explains results rather than predicting them before kick-off.

Method & caveats

Data

StatsBomb Open Data for La Liga, 2004/05 to 2020/21: every event and full lineups for 524 Barcelona matches. StatsBomb's free release covers the league games Lionel Messi played for Barça, so this is the Messi era, not every Barça match. Games he missed (injuries, rotation) aren't here.

Player stats

26 metrics per Barça player per match, converted to per-90 rates using minutes reconstructed from lineups (stoppage time included). Players under 30 minutes are dropped. Position is the one a player played longest that match. Goalkeepers are excluded.

Statistics

Spearman correlation between each stat and the result (loss 0, draw 0.5, win 1), with Benjamini–Hochberg correction across 26 tests per position group. The heatmap's shade is −log₁₀ of the adjusted p-value, so darker means stronger evidence.

The win model is a logistic regression on standardised Barça-minus-opponent stat differences plus a home/away flag. It's scored with leave-one-season-out cross-validation and compared with always predicting the win rate of the other seasons.

Caveats

Correlation isn't causation, and in-game stats are partly caused by the score: a team that's 2–0 up plays differently. Barça won most of these games, so "dropped points" is a small group (about one game in four).
